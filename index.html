Chapter 2

Simple, but working.

Three hours later there was a handful of small scripts, a dashboard, and an Excel export. It worked — faster and more easily than I'd expected.

At this point it was just a data collector. Fuel for the AI analysis that was supposed to come next.

Chapter 3

Something didn't add up.

Comparing files from different devices and years showed a pattern the collector script had nothing to do with. My current device consistently delivered full detail. My older devices varied wildly — sometimes close to that, sometimes barely a fraction of it.

high
~550 KB · my current device
standard
<1 KB – ~480 KB · varies across my older devices

Not a bug. My older devices simply carried fewer sensors — and never sent minute-by-minute detail to begin with.

Then came the harder discovery: it wasn't only about old devices. Even my current watch loses its full resolution — Garmin quietly reduces intraday detail after several months.

The path sharpened the idea.

What it does

A safeguard against data decay.

Preserve

Archive your data locally, for the long term — before Garmin's own history quietly loses detail. Along the way, it checks data quality and stops good data from ever being silently overwritten by worse data.

Contextualize

Combine Garmin data with local context — weather, pollen, and more.

Recover

Rebuild historical data from Garmin exports and fold it back into the archive.

Explore

Dashboards, Excel and HTML exports, a local AI chat, or MCP — use whichever fits best.

Local by design

Your data stays on your machine.

No extra account for GLA. No subscription. No vendor lock-in.

  • No data leaves your machine unless you export it yourself
  • The archive itself is plain, machine-readable data — not locked to GLA
  • Encrypted dashboards and an encrypted mirror container, for when you do want to move the archive between machines
